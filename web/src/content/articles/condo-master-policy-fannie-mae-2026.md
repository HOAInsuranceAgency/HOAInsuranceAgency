> Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac changed their condo Insurance rules in 2026. For condo master policies, roofs no longer have to be insured at replacement cost, the inflation guard requirement is gone, and per-unit deductibles now have a flat $50,000 cap. The master policy must still cover 100% of replacement cost. This guide explains what Fannie Mae's current Selling Guide requires and what boards should check before the next renewal.

## What changed for condo master policies in 2026

On March 18, 2026, Fannie Mae published [Lender Letter LL-2026-03](https://capitalmarkets.fanniemae.com/mortgage-backed-securities/single-family-mbs/fannie-mae-announces-updates-single-family-project-standards-and-property-insurance-requirements), which updated its condo project standards and its property Insurance requirements for project developments. Fannie Mae said the changes were made in alignment with Freddie Mac and in coordination with the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA). [FHFA](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-remove-certain-homeowners-insurance-requirements-that-will-reduce-costs), which regulates both companies, said condo buildings can now use actual cash value roof coverage and that the per-unit deductible rule had been simplified.

On August 5, 2026, [Fannie Mae's Announcement SEL-2026-07](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/announcement-sel-2026-07-selling-guide-updates) incorporated most of the lender letter's changes into the Selling Guide, which sets Fannie Mae's requirements for the loans it buys. It said the policy changes were already in effect under the lender letter unless otherwise noted. Freddie Mac revised its own guide in parallel, as [Orrick's InfoBytes summary](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-03-27/fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-revise-homeowners-insurance-and-condominium-requirements/) explains, so this article uses Fannie Mae's published text as the reference point.

These rules matter to any condo board whose buyers or owners use a mortgage that Fannie Mae purchases. Under [Fannie Mae's master property Insurance requirements (B7-3-03)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments), a condo project needs a master policy covering the common elements and residential structures for each loan Fannie Mae buys, unless the project's legal documents require individual policies for each unit. The lender or servicer must verify that coverage. Even when Fannie Mae waives the project review, as it does for some condo projects of ten units or fewer, its [waiver rules (B4-2.1-02)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-2.1-02/waiver-project-review) still require the applicable Insurance requirements to be met.

## Coverage amount: still 100% of replacement cost

Replacement cost is the cost to repair or replace damaged property without a deduction for depreciation, as the [NAIC explains](https://content.naic.org/article/rebuilding-after-storm-know-difference-between-replacement-cost-and-actual-cash-value-when-it-comes). [Fannie Mae's B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments) requires the master policy limit to equal at least 100% of the estimated replacement cost value of the project improvements, including both the common elements and the residential structures. The policy must cover all insurable property elements, and the association must pay the premium as a common expense.

The guide gives lenders five ways to confirm the limit is enough. A lender may rely on guaranteed replacement cost coverage, extended replacement cost coverage, a replacement cost estimate from the insurer, the project's Insurance risk appraisal, or a statement from the insurer or another qualified professional. Keeping at least one of these documents current makes lender questions easier to answer.

The 2026 update also removed the requirement for an inflation guard endorsement on master policies, according to both the [Community Associations Institute (CAI)](https://advocacy.caionline.org/fannie-freddie-update031826/) and the Texas law firm [Manning & Meyers](https://www.hoalegal.com/blog/client-alert-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-update-condominium-project-and-insurance-requirements-for-2026/). An inflation guard endorsement automatically adjusts the insured value of property using a formula in the policy, as a [legal opinion published by New York's Department of Financial Services](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/insurance/ogco2010/rg100911.htm) describes it. Without a required automatic adjustment, the board has more reason to keep its valuation current, because the 100% coverage requirement still applies.

## Roofs can now be insured at actual cash value

[B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments) now says the master policy must provide coverage on a replacement cost basis, with the exception of roofs. Roofs must still be insured, but not necessarily at replacement cost. The guide adds that some insurers provide actual cash value coverage for personal property and certain property elements, and that Fannie Mae accepts those terms.

Actual cash value (ACV) coverage pays the depreciated cost to repair or replace damaged property, according to the [NAIC](https://content.naic.org/article/rebuilding-after-storm-know-difference-between-replacement-cost-and-actual-cash-value-when-it-comes). The NAIC notes that insurers usually base depreciation on the property's condition at the time of loss, what a new item would cost and how long it would normally last. In the NAIC's own roof example, the same $15,000 of damage produces a $14,000 payment under replacement cost coverage but only $4,000 under ACV coverage after $10,000 of depreciation and a $1,000 deductible. For an association, that gap would come out of its own funds.

[FHFA](https://www.fhfa.gov/news/news-release/fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-remove-certain-homeowners-insurance-requirements-that-will-reduce-costs) described ACV roof coverage as the cheaper option. Because Fannie Mae accepts it, the choice becomes a budgeting decision for the board rather than a lender compliance issue. Before accepting an ACV roof provision at renewal, boards should compare the premium saving with the age of their roofs, the reserve balance set aside for roof replacement and whether the governing documents or state law ask for more than the lender minimum.

## Deductibles: the 5% limit and the $50,000 per-unit cap

A deductible is the part of a claim the policyholder pays out of pocket, as the [NAIC](https://content.naic.org/article/rebuilding-after-storm-know-difference-between-replacement-cost-and-actual-cash-value-when-it-comes) puts it. Under [B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments), the maximum per-occurrence deductible for the required perils is 5% of the master policy coverage amount. If the policy has separate deductibles for different perils, such as windstorm or wildfire, each one must meet that limit. For example, on a coverage amount of $20 million, the 5% limit works out to $1 million per occurrence.

Where a master policy uses a per-unit deductible, [B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments) caps it at $50,000 per unit for all required perils. When the master policy has a per-unit deductible, each borrower must carry a unit owners policy. The [Community Associations Institute](https://advocacy.caionline.org/fannie-freddie-update031826/) reported that the $50,000 cap applies beginning July 1, and [Manning & Meyers](https://www.hoalegal.com/blog/client-alert-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-update-condominium-project-and-insurance-requirements-for-2026/) likewise put the compliance date at July 1, 2026.

The [Community Associations Institute](https://advocacy.caionline.org/fannie-freddie-update031826/) warned that the new cap may create compliance challenges for communities that carry higher deductibles. The guide offers one route: a deductible buy-back policy bought by the association can be used to meet the maximum deductible requirements. [B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments) allows this only if the buy-back policy meets all other property Insurance requirements, including the insurer rating requirements.

## Perils, endorsements and extra coverages lenders check

[B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments) says master policies should be written on a "special" coverage form or its equivalent. At a minimum, coverage must include the perils of a commercial "broad" form, which the guide lists as including fire, windstorm (including named storms), hail, sprinkler leakage, weight of snow, ice or sleet, and water damage. If the master policy excludes or limits a required peril, the association must obtain an acceptable policy for it, such as a stand-alone policy.

Condo master policies must also carry a Condominium Association Coverage Form or equivalent endorsement. Under [B7-3-03](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-03/master-property-insurance-requirements-project-developments), it must recognize an Insurance trustee if the association names one, waive the insurer's right to recover payment from unit owners and state that the master policy is primary, not contributing with an owner's own Insurance on the same property.

Two other coverages appear in the same section. Building ordinance or law coverage must include loss to the undamaged portion of a building, demolition costs and increased costs of construction, unless it is not obtainable in the Insurance market available to the association. Equipment breakdown coverage is required when the project has central heating or cooling, with a limit equal to the lesser of $2 million or the replacement cost of the building housing the equipment.

## What unit owners need alongside the master policy

[Fannie Mae's unit requirements (B7-3-04)](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-04/individual-property-insurance-requirements-unit-project-development) require a unit owners policy when any part of the unit's interior or improvements is not covered by the master policy, or when the master policy has a per-unit deductible. The [Community Associations Institute](https://advocacy.caionline.org/fannie-freddie-update031826/) notes that this is usually an HO-6 policy.

That owner policy must be at least the greater of the amount needed to restore the parts of the unit the master policy does not cover, or the amount of the per-unit deductible. If the per-unit deductible applies to a specific required peril, the owner's policy must cover that peril. [B7-3-04](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b7-3-04/individual-property-insurance-requirements-unit-project-development) also requires replacement cost loss settlement and limits the owner's deductible to the greater of 5% of the coverage amount or $2,500.

Boards can make this easier by telling owners in writing what the master policy covers and what the deductibles are. Owners can then match their own coverage to the gap. Our [condo Insurance page](/condo-insurance/) explains how master and unit coverage fit together.

## A year-end review for boards

Budget and renewal season is a good time to compare the master policy's declarations page with each requirement above. Confirm the coverage amount against a current valuation, check how roofs are valued, list every deductible and confirm any per-unit deductible is $50,000 or less. Then check that the condo endorsement, ordinance or law coverage and, if needed, equipment breakdown coverage are in place.

Lenders are also looking at more than Insurance. [Fannie Mae's SEL-2026-07](https://singlefamily.fanniemae.com/media/document/pdf/announcement-sel-2026-07-selling-guide-updates) says new reserve standards go into effect for new loan applications on January 4, 2027. The [Community Associations Institute](https://advocacy.caionline.org/fannie-freddie-update031826/) and [Manning & Meyers](https://www.hoalegal.com/blog/client-alert-fannie-mae-and-freddie-mac-update-condominium-project-and-insurance-requirements-for-2026/) both report that the minimum reserve allocation rises from 10% to 15% of the annual budget. CAI adds that the 15% allocation is not required if the association has a reserve study conducted or updated within the last three years and follows its highest recommended funding level, with baseline funding not allowed.

Both sources also report that the limited review process was retired for loan applications dated on or after August 3, 2026. CAI says this shifts more sales to full reviews, with more formal lender questionnaires for associations to complete.
