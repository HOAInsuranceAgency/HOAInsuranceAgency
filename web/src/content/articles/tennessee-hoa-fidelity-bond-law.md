> From January 1, 2027, every Tennessee homeowners association and condominium association that collects assessments must carry a blanket fidelity bond, or an Insurance policy that does the same job. The limit must equal the association's reserves plus one-fourth of its annual assessment income, and it can never be less than $10,000. This guide explains who the Tennessee law covers, how to work out your limit and what to check before the deadline.

## What Tennessee's new HOA fidelity bond law requires

Tennessee boards have a new Insurance requirement arriving with the new year. Senate Bill 2326 cleared the Tennessee General Assembly with final passage in the House on March 26, 2026, according to [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) and [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/). Its House companion, House Bill 2338, is listed by [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1961481) with the note that the companion bill became Public Chapter 731. Governor Bill Lee signed the law on April 23, 2026, and it takes effect January 1, 2027, as [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/), [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) and [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) report.

The core rule is short. According to the as-enacted summary of the bill posted by [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1961481), and as [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) also reports, a homeowners association that collects assessments for common expenses must obtain and maintain a blanket fidelity bond. The bond must insure the association against losses from theft or dishonesty by its officers, directors or employees, or by a managing agent or the managing agent's employees.

Two terms are worth defining. A fidelity bond protects the association when someone trusted with its money steals it, as [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) puts it. [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) describes a managing agent as the property management company itself, and the company's employees often include the bookkeeper or community manager handling the association's account.

The law also sets a minimum amount. [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) reports that the bond or Insurance policy must equal the association's reserve balances plus one-fourth of its aggregate annual assessment income, with a floor of $10,000. [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) describes the same formula and minimum, and both sources say the board of directors or the managing agent may buy the coverage on the association's behalf.

## Why Tennessee lawmakers acted

The bill followed allegations that a Middle Tennessee property management company took money from homeowners associations it managed, as [SuretyBonds.com](https://www.suretybonds.com/blog/new-tennessee-bill-hoa-fidelity-bond/) and [Surety One](https://suretyone.com/blog/the-tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-a-new-statutory-mandate-for-community-association-crime-protection/) both describe. Those are allegations, not court findings, but they help explain why the law reaches managers and their staff and not only volunteer board members.

The episode also shows why the type of coverage matters. [Surety One's analysis](https://suretyone.com/blog/the-tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-a-new-statutory-mandate-for-community-association-crime-protection/) makes the point that a general property and liability policy is not a substitute for fidelity coverage, because crime coverage is what responds to dishonesty by trusted insiders. In other words, a property and liability program built around the buildings and common areas is not the coverage designed to respond when an insider diverts association funds.

## Which Tennessee associations must comply

The requirement reaches beyond single-family subdivisions. The law's definition of a homeowners association expressly includes a unit owners' association organized under Tennessee Code Section 66-27-401, so condominium associations must comply too, as both [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) and [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) explain.

The trigger is collecting assessments for common expenses, not the size of the community. [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) notes that a small neighborhood with modest annual dues falls under the same rule as a large master-planned community, and that townhome communities are included when owners pay assessments to an association. Because the limit follows a formula, what changes from one association to the next is the required amount, not whether the rule applies.

Boards with questions about how the law applies to their own declaration or bylaws should also involve association counsel.

## Who the bond must cover

The law names five groups whose dishonesty the bond must cover: the association's officers, its directors, the people it employs, its managing agent and the managing agent's employees. That list matches across the as-enacted bill summary on [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/billdetail/1961481) and the coverage of the law by [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx).

The last two groups deserve the closest look. [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) cautions that a basic crime policy may cover only the association's own employees, which is where many existing policies fall short. Boards that use a management company should confirm that the coverage names the manager and its staff, the same source advises.

The practical step is to ask your agent to confirm, in writing, that each of the five groups named in the law falls within the policy's definition of who is covered. If the management company carries its own crime coverage, ask whether it names your association and meets the formula, and have association counsel confirm whether it satisfies the association's own obligation before relying on it.

## How to calculate the required limit

The formula uses two figures from the association's books. Reserve balances are the funds set aside for the future repair and replacement of major components such as roofs, paving and elevators. Aggregate annual assessment income is the total the association collects from owners in assessments over a year.

Here is how the arithmetic works, using made-up numbers. An association with $250,000 in reserves and $400,000 in annual assessment income would add one-fourth of $400,000, or $100,000, to its reserves, for a minimum limit of $350,000. A small association with $8,000 in reserves and $6,000 in annual dues would calculate $9,500, so the $10,000 floor reported by [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) and [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) would apply instead.

The required limit is not fixed. [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) points out that it changes every time reserves or dues change, so a bond that complies in January can fall short by the end of the year. The same source suggests setting a yearly reminder to recheck the limit as reserves grow.

The summaries of the law cited in this article do not say on which date reserve balances should be measured, or whether special assessments count toward annual assessment income. Boards should put both questions to association counsel, then set the limit with some room above the result so that normal reserve growth during the policy term does not leave the association short.

## Bond or crime Insurance policy: checking what you already have

Many boards will find they already carry some form of this coverage. Both [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/new-tennessee-laws-mandate-fidelity-bonds-for-hoas-restructure-insurance-committees-573306.aspx) and [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) describe the requirement as satisfied by a bond or an Insurance policy, and Hutsenpiller notes that a commercial crime policy written for the association can meet it if the limit and the covered parties match the law.

So the review starts with the current policy itself, not with a new purchase. Compare its limit with your formula result, check who it covers against the five groups in the law, and confirm the association is the named insured, a point [Surety One](https://suretyone.com/blog/the-tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-a-new-statutory-mandate-for-community-association-crime-protection/) also stresses. If the limit is short, ask whether it can be raised on the existing policy or whether a separate bond is needed.

It also helps to understand how these policies respond to a loss. [Surety One](https://suretyone.com/blog/the-tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-a-new-statutory-mandate-for-community-association-crime-protection/) explains that underwriters typically write these bonds on a discovery form, meaning the policy responds to losses discovered during the bond period, provided the dishonest act took place after a stated retroactive date. Ask your agent which form your coverage uses and what retroactive date applies. At one affected Tennessee community, the suspect payments Surety One describes from local news reporting were spread over a 42-month period.

## What boards should do before January 1, 2027

With the effective date only weeks away, the first step is to gather the two numbers the formula needs: the current reserve balance and the total annual assessment income in the adopted 2027 budget. [Hutsenpiller Insurance](https://hutins.com/posts/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-what-boards-must-do-by-january-1-2027) advises starting early because bonds take time to underwrite.

Next, send those figures and your current crime or fidelity policy to your agent and ask for a written comparison against the Tennessee requirement. Hutsenpiller also recommends adding the bond premium to the 2027 budget. Treat it as a recurring cost rather than a one-time expense, since the law requires associations to obtain and maintain the coverage.

Finally, record the decision. A short note in the board minutes stating the formula result, the limit purchased, the policy period and who confirmed the covered parties gives future boards and owners a clear record. Because either the board or the managing agent may buy the coverage under the law, as [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) notes, the minutes should also state who is responsible for renewing it.

## What Tennessee unit owners should know

Owners have a direct stake in this law. [All Seasons Insurance Group](https://asigtn.com/blog/tennessee-hoa-fidelity-bond-law-january-1-2027/) explains that when association money disappears, the shortfall usually comes back to owners as a special assessment, and that a properly sized fidelity bond at the association level is the first line of defense.

An owner's own condo policy, often called an HO-6, may include some loss assessment coverage. [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/loss-assessment) defines a loss assessment as an owner's share of a loss to property owned in common by the members of an association. It notes that condominium unit owners policies typically include a small amount of this coverage, with more available by endorsement. All Seasons cautions that limits and covered causes vary by policy form, so owners should read their policy rather than assume it will respond to an assessment caused by theft. Our [condo Insurance page](/condo-insurance/) explains how the master policy and an owner's coverage fit together.

Owners can also simply ask the board whether the association has confirmed it meets the new Tennessee standard. A board that can answer with the formula result, the limit purchased and the policy period has a clear record to show for its work before January 1, 2027.
