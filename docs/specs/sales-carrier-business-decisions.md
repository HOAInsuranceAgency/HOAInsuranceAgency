# Confirmed business decisions

September 10, 2026. Companion to the [revision specification](sales-carrier-workflow-revision.md). **The owner has resolved these six policies. They are specification decisions, not implemented or deployed behavior.** The owner's annual incumbent-date rollover replaces the earlier proposal for a generic “Revisit at renewal” action.

## Established responsibilities

Salespeople manage leads only and escalate to their assigned sales manager. Champions own carrier work and become the client's main contact after binding, including direct renewal contact and coordination with specialists. Champion work escalates to the designated carrier/marketing manager. Both types of manager escalate unhandled work to the agency owner. Prospect follow-ups use two-business-day intervals for the first two reminders, then weekly. Scheduled reminders and reports are at 9 a.m. Eastern. Actual work supplies the record, and Front cleanup never changes a business commitment.

## 1. Client service after binding

The champion remains the client's main contact after binding and coordinates certificates, policy changes, billing questions, claims routing and other specialist work with the appropriate people. Sales stays focused on leads. A specialist receives a named piece of work; simply forwarding to an inbox does not remove the champion's responsibility to coordinate the client's answer. Preserve unfinished requests and document delivery during the bind handoff.

## 2. Renewal preparation and quote targets

Begin preparation **90 calendar days before expiration**, follow carrier-specific submission deadlines and target usable quotes **14 calendar days before expiration**. These are agency planning targets, not carrier guarantees or changes to coverage terms. Start earlier where an existing carrier requirement demands it.

The preparation checkpoint and carrier submission deadlines remain distinct. Use the existing quote/submission requirements for new-business marketing, without a separate handoff form. Missing prospect information belongs to sales; missing bound-client information belongs to the champion. A draft or submitted quote does not count as a usable received quote, and quotes must match the actual risk, term and lines. Late-arriving opportunities show their shortened timeline rather than false historical staff failures.

## 3. Routine response and follow-up targets

- First contact for a manually captured/imported lead with no recorded contact: **within one business day**.
- Present a usable quote after it is ready: **within one business day**; sales handles new business and champions handle bound-client renewals.
- Carrier and renewal-information follow-ups: **after two business days** without a response, preserving earlier fixed requirements.
- Prospect update while carrier work progresses: **weekly**, measured as five business days without a substantive human update.
- Client/carrier response and callback work: retain the established **one-business-day** response rule with the responsible sales or champion owner.

Normal substantive communication satisfies overlapping updates. Do not require an extra status email, action-completion form or separate documentation. Keep the existing website AI-introduction timing and agreed prospect follow-up cadence.

## 4. Unanswered calls count as outreach attempts

A genuine completed outbound attempt satisfies the obligation to try contacting the person. Record it automatically and schedule the next attempt. It does not prove the call connected, the question was answered, a document arrived or a business promise was fulfilled.

Separate outreach timeliness from request resolution. Do not report employee failure solely because the other person did not answer. Escalate missed required attempts and still-unfulfilled business obligations. The first retry after an unanswered missed-call return attempt is the next business morning; ordinary prospect cadence then resumes. Preserve the original request and real promise dates.

Cleanup can close work that is waiting on the other party when a durable next attempt exists and no immediate requirement blocks closing. Drafts, provider failures, an opened dialer or an incoming missed call are not completed outbound attempts.

## 5. Next year: move incumbent expiration, then catch the lead

The owner's direction is to **advance the incumbent expiration exactly one calendar year**, then remind the salesperson **90 calendar days before that new date**. This is a lead-cycle change, not a request for the agent to set an action date.

A deliberate **Next year** control updates the lead's incumbent date, records the old/new values automatically, pauses ordinary chasing for that deferred opportunity and establishes the calculated future return. Preserve lead ownership, source and history. Display the new expiration and return date in Upcoming. Reopen salesperson work at 9 a.m. on the calculated business morning, with sales-manager and owner escalation if the required outreach is missed.

Example: **December 1, 2026 → December 1, 2027 → salesperson reminder September 2, 2027 at 9 a.m. Eastern**, assuming the normal calendar. No manual due-date choice is needed.

Use calendar-year arithmetic, not 365 elapsed days. February 29 rolls to February 28 in a non-leap year. A weekend/holiday return threshold uses the preceding business morning. Missing dates require the actual business fact; stale dates do not cause silent repeated year increments. Duplicate requests cannot advance the lead twice. The independent coverage check must recover a missed return without recreating early weekly chasing.

New substantive messages, separate promises and active bind/coverage obligations remain actionable. Only the deliberately deferred cycle's obsolete targets are superseded; historical quotes and submissions retain their actual outcomes. Update the lead's planning date, not an issued policy's coverage dates. No background job automatically rolls a silent lead forward another year.

## 6. Managers escalate to the owner

Both sales managers and the carrier/marketing manager escalate unhandled work to the agency owner. Retain the **one-business-day manager recovery window** and the **9 a.m.** schedule: a manager escalation on Monday morning becomes an owner escalation Tuesday morning if the required work is still unhandled, absent a holiday.

Acknowledging, forwarding or reassigning does not reset the original deadline or the owner-escalation clock. Distinguish actual outreach attempts, external waiting and delivery failures from employee inaction. Combine exceptions into morning reports and continue visibility until recovered; avoid duplicate/self-escalation when one person fills multiple roles.

Managers arrange eligible temporary coverage once in Team settings when someone is away. Missing coverage becomes an owned exception rather than a reason to pause obligations.

## Remaining setup

Populate each salesperson's manager, the designated marketing manager, agency owner/fallback, temporary coverage, an owner for unmatched incoming activity and an administrator for integration failures. Validate the business calendar and verified staff delivery destinations. Preserve the chosen default lead assignments unless the owner changes them.

No additional business-policy choices remain from this decision pass. Implementation and staging acceptance must now follow the revised specification, particularly annual rollover, successful call-attempt credit, manager-to-owner escalation and champion ownership after binding.
